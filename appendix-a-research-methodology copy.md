# Appendix A: Research Methodology

*The Modern Organic Growth Playbook: How the best B2B brands build durable demand in 2026*

---

This appendix documents how the 118-company cohort was selected, measured, classified, and analyzed. It also summarizes the study's quality controls. Metric formulas appear in Appendix C.

---

## Research Objective

This study examined one question:

*What distinguished B2B SaaS companies that continued growing organic traffic during a period of expanding AI visibility and algorithm volatility from those that stalled or declined?*

The research used an observational longitudinal design across an 18-month study window. It identifies recurring associations rather than experimental causality. The consistency of the findings across companies, categories, and analytical methods provides strong support for the report's directional conclusions.

## Cohort Design and Selection

The study used a category-stratified cohort of B2B SaaS companies with sufficient operating and organic search history to support an 18-month analysis.

Approximately 20–30 candidates were identified in each category before screening. Candidates were sourced primarily through G2 category pages, with Crozdesk, GetApp, and Capterra used when additional candidates were needed. These directories were used for discovery only. Their rankings and quality labels were not included in the analysis.

**Ten categories**

1. Sales Engagement / Sales Intelligence
2. Marketing Automation
3. Customer Support / Help Desk
4. Customer Success
5. HR / People Operations
6. Finance / Spend Management
7. Project Management / Collaboration
8. Developer Tools / DevOps
9. Security / Compliance
10. Product Analytics / Product Management

Category was used for stratified company selection and within-category comparison. It did not determine a company's traffic classification or content-pattern classification.

**Inclusion gates**

A company was included only when it met all five requirements:

1. Its primary business was a B2B SaaS product.
2. It had an independently interpretable operating history.
3. Its primary domain and operations remained sufficiently continuous during the study window.
4. Its core product clearly fit the category being studied.
5. Ahrefs reported trackable organic traffic at both ends of the study window.

Employee count was recorded but not used for inclusion.

Every inclusion and exclusion decision was preserved in the screening archive with the specific gate and rationale that determined it. Companies affected by acquisitions, domain migrations, operating discontinuity, or ambiguous category fit were excluded when those conditions prevented a defensible comparison.

**Three analytical phases**

- Phase 1: Candidate screening and cohort selection
- Phase 2: Full-cohort measurement and traffic classification
- Phase 3: Company deep dives, comparison briefs, and AI citation validation

---

## Measurement and Traffic Classification

Organic performance was measured across the 18-month window from January 2025 through June 2026. Three-month averages were used at both ends of the window to reduce monthly volatility. All reported changes use these averages unless otherwise noted.

Traffic change = (end-period average − start-period average) ÷ start-period average × 100

Start period: January–March 2025
End period: April–June 2026

**Primary traffic classifications**

| Classification | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Grew | > +10% |
| Flat | −10% to +10% |
| Declined | < −10% |

Thresholds were locked before classification began and applied uniformly across all 118 companies. Manual review was used only when a near-zero starting value made percentage change misleading. Every override was documented.

**Final distribution:** 44 grew · 19 remained flat · 55 declined

**Full-cohort fields**

For every company, the study recorded:

*Performance*
- Organic traffic and change
- Traffic value and change
- Referring domains and change

*Peak context*
- Study-window peak and month
- Historical peak and month
- Distance from historical peak

*Classification*
- Grew / Flat / Declined
- Traffic trajectory

Phase 2 used only standardized company-level Ahrefs measurements. Page-, query-, and position-level exports were introduced during Phase 3.

**Traffic trajectory taxonomy**

Trajectory labels describe traffic shape only. They do not affect a company's Grew, Flat, or Declined classification.

Nine trajectory labels were used: Continuous Acceleration, Steady Growth, Growth then Pullback, Early Peak then Plateau, Early Peak then Decline, Decline then Recovery, Flat, Steady Decline, and Volatile.

## Deep-Dive Analysis and Mechanism Attribution

Fifteen companies received structured Phase 3 deep dives. Selection was designed to cover five recurring questions:

1. How do successful programs continue accelerating?
2. Why do previously successful programs stall or decline?
3. How do companies recover after sustained decline?
4. Why do some companies fail to recover?
5. How do companies break out of prolonged stagnation?

The set deliberately included growing, flat, and declining companies. Declining companies were included to distinguish durable growth from conditions that only appeared healthy.

**Standardized analysis**

Every deep dive used the same 14-field framework covering performance data, historical context, page-level analysis, authority signals, algorithm and AI timing, competing explanations, and final mechanism attribution.

Each analysis included an explicit competing-explanations step before a mechanism was assigned. Funding events, product launches, category shifts, acquisitions, domain changes, competitor exits, and other off-content explanations were evaluated rather than assumed away.

**Standardized external timelines**

Verified timelines of major Google algorithm updates and AI search expansion events were compiled before company-level synthesis and applied consistently across all deep dives. This allowed the study to compare company inflection points against external events without retroactively selecting dates to fit individual cases.

**Comparison briefs**

Nine comparison briefs examined companies facing similar category conditions but producing divergent outcomes. Each followed the same structure: shared context → divergent trajectory → mechanism comparison → cross-company conclusion. These comparisons strengthened the analysis by showing which differences persisted among companies operating in similar category conditions.

## Content Growth-Pattern Classification and AI Citation Testing

**Content growth-pattern classification**

After the deep dives, all 44 growing companies were reviewed to identify the content pattern contributing most strongly to their observed organic growth during the study window.

Five primary patterns were used: definitional and educational content, brand and product demand, two confirmed co-primary patterns, programmatic content, and interactive tools and free resources.

Deep-dive companies were classified from the full Phase 3 evidence. Remaining companies were assessed through site architecture, representative pages, and Ahrefs Top Pages exports where needed. Classification was assigned to the content pattern that contributed most meaningfully to both traffic composition and growth during the study window.

Of the 44 growing companies: 38 were classifiable at full weight; 2 were classifiable but treated as low-weight evidence due to limited traffic scale; and 4 were excluded from pattern synthesis because technical artifacts or unit-of-analysis problems prevented a defensible classification. The distribution reported in the main study uses the 38 full-weight cases.

**AI citation validation**

AI citation behavior was tested for 13 of the 15 deep-dive companies using Scrunch across six engines: ChatGPT, Copilot, Google AI Mode, Google AI Overviews, Gemini, and Perplexity. Hyperproof and LogRocket were added to the deep-dive cohort after citation testing was complete.

Prompts reflected each company's category, core use cases, and likely citation queries. Results were recorded by prompt, engine, company, and cited domain.

The testing was used to evaluate observed citation patterns and structural routing behavior. Because AI retrieval systems change continuously, these results represent patterns observed during the testing window, not a permanent citation state.

## Data Sources and Quality Controls

**Data sources**

| Source | Use in the study |
|---|---|
| Ahrefs | Organic traffic, traffic value, referring domains, rankings, and Top Pages |
| Google Search Console | Branded/non-branded and CTR analysis for the limited cases where first-party access was available |
| G2 and supplementary directories | Candidate discovery only |
| LinkedIn | Standardized employee-count metadata |
| Crunchbase | Founding, funding, ownership, and M&A context |
| Scrunch | AI citation testing across six engines |

**Interpreting Ahrefs estimates**

Ahrefs traffic figures are modeled estimates rather than first-party session data. The study therefore emphasizes directional movement, relative change, position distribution, and cross-company differences rather than treating estimated traffic as an exact session count.

Larger differences, repeated patterns across companies, and findings supported by multiple Ahrefs reports provide stronger evidence than isolated changes in a single estimated metric.

**Quality controls**

The following controls were applied across the research process:

- Inclusion and exclusion decisions were documented before analysis began.
- Classification thresholds were locked before the cohort distribution was known.
- The same formulas were applied uniformly across all 118 companies.
- Every manual classification override was recorded.
- All 15 deep dives used the same 14-field framework without selective abbreviation.
- Every deep dive required evaluation of competing explanations before mechanism assignment.
- Algorithm update and AI search expansion timelines were compiled before synthesis.
- Cross-company conclusions were developed only after all individual analyses and comparison briefs were complete.
- Low-scale, technically distorted, or unclassifiable cases were prevented from carrying full interpretive weight in the synthesis.

## Scope and Interpretation

**Observational evidence**

The study identifies consistent associations, not experimental causality. Its conclusions are strongest where the same pattern appeared across multiple companies, categories, and analytical views.

**Modeled search data**

Ahrefs estimates are best used to compare direction, composition, and relative magnitude. Individual traffic figures should not be interpreted as exact first-party session counts.

**Defined market sample**

The cohort consists of B2B SaaS companies discoverable through major software directories and with sufficient organic presence for Ahrefs to model. The findings are most applicable to comparable B2B software companies and may not extend directly to very early-stage businesses or sites with little measurable search presence.

**Defined study period**

The research reflects the search environment from January 2025 through June 2026. Specific AI features and retrieval behavior will continue evolving. The durable-demand framework is based on whether content requires continued engagement to deliver value. That structural property does not depend on the permanence of any individual search interface.

**Time-boxed company analysis**

Each deep dive used a standardized analysis process. This supported consistent cohort-scale comparison but cannot reproduce the depth of a full internal audit with first-party analytics, conversion data, and stakeholder interviews.

**AI citation snapshot**

Citation results describe behavior during the testing period. They should be read as evidence of observed patterns, not guaranteed future visibility.

**Category coverage**

All ten categories were represented in the full-cohort classification. Nine received Phase 3 deep dives or comparison briefs. Project Management / Collaboration was represented in the classification data but not in the Phase 3 mechanism analysis.

---

Within these boundaries, the study provides a consistent account of why organic outcomes diverged across 118 B2B SaaS companies and which content, portfolio, and visibility patterns most clearly distinguished durable growth from fragile traffic.
