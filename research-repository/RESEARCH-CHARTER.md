# S.LAB Research Charter

*The foundational document for the Organic Growth Research Repository. Every field, workflow, taxonomy, and analysis in this repository should be traceable to the mission, objectives, and principles defined here. When in doubt about whether something belongs, apply the research test and the client test at the bottom of this document.*

---

## Mission

Build an evidence-backed understanding of what makes organic growth durable in an AI-first internet, then use that evidence to diagnose struggling websites and prescribe the highest-confidence path to sustainable recovery.

---

## Vision

The repository's long-term purpose is portfolio engineering — not recovery consulting.

Recovery consulting starts with a client whose traffic is declining. Portfolio engineering starts earlier: analyzing any organic program — growing or declining — and identifying which assets are structurally durable, which are fragile, and how to rebalance before decline occurs.

The most valuable client engagement looks like this:

> "Your traffic is growing. But 63% of it comes from highly AI-substitutable content. Based on patterns we've observed across comparable portfolios, this kind of concentration historically precedes decline. Here's how we'd rebalance over the next 12 months."

That is only possible when the repository contains enough evidence to support probabilistic statements about portfolio composition and risk. We are building toward that capability. Everything in the repository should move us closer to it.

---

## The Research Question

Organic growth research has traditionally focused on ranking factors — what causes a page to rank at a point in time. This repository asks a different question:

**What makes organic growth durable?**

A page can rank today through excellent links, on-page optimization, and topical authority — and lose most of that traffic in a single algorithm update, AI Overview rollout, or shift in how users find information. The research question is not "what makes a page rank?" but "what makes a page continue producing value after algorithms change, AI systems emerge, and search behavior shifts?"

These are different questions that require different evidence. Ranking factors and durability factors overlap, but they are not the same. This distinction is the foundation of everything in this repository.

---

## Four Objectives

### Objective 1 — Understand what actually happened
Company-level descriptive analysis: trajectory, mechanism, AI exposure, and confidence level.

Questions this answers: Why did this company grow? Why did it decline? What mechanism was responsible? Was AI involved? Was it algorithmic reassessment, demand expansion, concentration risk, AI substitution?

*This is where company records live.*

### Objective 2 — Discover what makes content durable
Page-level analysis: which content archetypes survived, recovered, or were abandoned during portfolio reassessment events? Which pages earned AI citations? Which recovered without content changes? Which only recovered after structural intervention?

Questions this answers: Are commercial methodology pages more durable than generic informational pages? Does AI substitutability predict survival? What structural characteristics differentiate resilient pages from fragile ones?

*This is where the Durable Demand Framework is tested — not merely proposed.*

### Objective 3 — Turn observations into evidence-backed principles
Synthesis: when the same pattern appears across enough methodologically consistent analyses, it becomes a candidate principle. These principles are not asserted — they are earned through repeated observation and accumulated evidence.

Principles look like: "Commercial methodology pages consistently survived algorithmic reassessment across 11 companies regardless of whether content was visibly changed." That statement requires 11 companies' worth of evidence behind it. We build toward it; we do not shortcut to it.

*This is where the Evidence Register drives framework evolution.*

### Objective 4 — Turn principles into repeatable client prescriptions
Application: the diagnosis → evidence matching → prescription pipeline.

A client presents with declining traffic. The workflow:
1. **Diagnose:** mechanism, AI exposure, durable assets, fragile assets, page archetypes
2. **Match evidence:** "We've observed this pattern across 14 comparable companies"
3. **Prescribe:** "Based on that evidence, here are the interventions with the highest historical success rate"

Not best practices. Evidence.

*This is where the repository becomes a strategic planning tool.*

## Research Pipeline

```
Observation → Evidence → Pattern → Prediction → Prescription
```

**Observation** — Company records, page-level analysis (Pass 1/2/3), Wayback comparisons, keyword behavior, SERP replacement analysis. The raw material.

**Portfolio Durability Analysis** — For companies with a meaningful portfolio redistribution event, a companion document (`portfolio-durability/company-name.md`) that structures the page-level findings into recovery cohort, decline cohort, and optional stable cohort, with archetype classification, AI substitutability, Durable Demand Signals, and Outcome (Promote/Preserve/Abandon) for each page. This is where the question shifts from "what happened to this company?" to "which pages did Google choose to preserve, promote, or abandon — and what distinguished them?" The portfolio analysis is what makes Objective 2 (discover what makes content durable) operational and structured.

**Evidence** — Findings from Portfolio Durability Analyses, logged in the Evidence Register with stated strength, strategic importance, and explicit linkage to the Durable Demand Framework. Individual observations become evidence when they are documented with sufficient methodological rigor to be replicable.

**Pattern** — Multiple evidence items across methodologically consistent analyses, from different companies and content architectures, converge on a consistent finding. A candidate principle emerges.

**Prediction** — Pattern density becomes sufficient to support probabilistic statements: "Pages with this archetype and AI substitutability classification historically recovered at X% across N comparable companies."

**Prescription** — Predictions inform specific, evidence-matched client recommendations with stated confidence levels.

**Current position:** The repository operationalizes Observation → Evidence reliably. Pattern formation is beginning. Prediction and Prescription are Phase 3 and Phase 4 objectives — they require the Evidence Register to mature across a much larger cohort.

## Prescription Rule — Evidence-Backed Recommendations

Recommendations are an emergent output of accumulated evidence, not a property of any single case study. A recommendation derived from one company is a hypothesis. A recommendation derived from many is evidence-backed guidance.

Recommendations carry their own confidence level derived from the strength of the underlying evidence:

| Recommendation Confidence | Evidence Requirement | Practical meaning |
|--------------------------|---------------------|-------------------|
| **Emerging** | One or more Emerging evidence entries directly supporting the recommendation; no contradictory evidence | A working hypothesis — worth applying cautiously and flagging as preliminary |
| **Moderate** | Two or more consistent evidence entries, at least one at Replicating strength, across different companies and architectures | Pattern is visible; applies reliably within stated conditions |
| **Strong** | One or more entries at Established strength directly supporting the recommendation | Pattern is robust; high confidence within stated conditions |

**Where recommendations live:** In the Evidence Register — not in company records. Company records document observations, evidence, and confidence. The Evidence Register synthesizes them. Recommendations that cannot cite specific Evidence Register IDs are not recommendations; they are opinions.

**What prevents drift:** The confidence level of any recommendation is reassessed whenever the underlying evidence entries are updated. If a supporting evidence entry is challenged or superseded, the recommendation is immediately flagged for review. The evidence and the recommendation are co-located, so the derivation is always auditable.

## North Star Questions

Every company record, page analysis, portfolio comparison, and synthesis artifact should contribute to answering one or more of these four questions:

1. **What happened?** *(Descriptive)* — e.g., "Gong recovered +34.1% from trough to current."

2. **Why did it happen?** *(Diagnostic)* — e.g., "Recovery concentrated in commercial methodology pages; homepage growth was driven by increased branded demand rather than organic content gains."

3. **What does this teach us about durable organic growth?** *(Research)* — e.g., "Commercial methodology pages repeatedly survive algorithmic reassessment without requiring visible content rewrites."

4. **How should this change what we build for clients?** *(Application)* — e.g., "Prioritize preserving and deepening high-quality commercial methodology clusters before investing in generic informational content."

**If a field, workflow, or analysis cannot be traced to one of these four questions, reconsider whether it belongs in the repository.**

## Guiding Principles

**Evidence before conclusions.** Do not name a mechanism until the evidence supports it. Plausibility is not evidence. State what is observed, what is ruled out, and what remains hypothetical — always in that order.

**Durability, not rankings.** The research question is survival across change events, not ranking at a point in time. Outputs that describe ranking factors without connecting to durability serve a different research program.

**Observation before synthesis.** Do not promote a finding to a pattern until enough methodologically consistent observations exist. The failure mode is premature synthesis — confident-sounding conclusions built on insufficient evidence.

**Separate observable from interpretive.** Recovery Pattern is observable. Candidate Driver is interpretive. AI Substitutability is structural. These are different types of claims with different evidentiary standards. Never let interpretive language migrate into fields designed for observable facts.

**Preserve uncertainty honestly.** Medium confidence is not a failure — it is accurate calibration. High-confidence findings that cannot be defended erode the repository's value for clients. State what you know and what you don't.

**Importance is not the same as strength.** A finding can be well-replicated but strategically minor. A finding can be foundational but still early-stage. Rate both independently.

## Evidence Standards

Evidence items in the Evidence Register are classified on two independent dimensions.

### Evidence Strength — how well-established is the finding?

| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| **Emerging** | Observed in 1–2 companies with consistent methodology. No contradictory evidence. Candidate for replication. |
| **Replicating** | Observed in 3–5 companies with consistent mechanism classification and methodological discipline. Pattern is visible but not yet robust. |
| **Established** | Observed in 6+ companies. Pattern holds across different site architectures, content models, and categories. Disconfirming evidence has been considered and addressed. |

### Strategic Importance — how much does this matter for client recommendations?

| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| **Supporting** | Interesting but minor. Adds context; unlikely to change core recommendations on its own. |
| **Important** | Meaningful. Should inform recommendations in relevant situations. Would change the emphasis or sequencing of a recommendation. |
| **Foundational** | Core. Changes how we think about durability or how we approach client work. A client recommendation built without this insight would be materially weaker. |

Both dimensions are assigned independently. Never conflate strength with importance. A Foundational finding that is still Emerging needs to be flagged as such — not promoted prematurely.

---

## Framework Connection

Every evidence item in the Evidence Register must be explicitly linked to the Durable Demand Framework — which principle it strengthens, which it weakens, or whether it creates a new candidate principle.

Evidence that cannot be linked to the framework is either:
1. A candidate for a new framework principle — document the hypothesis explicitly
2. A peripheral finding — belongs in the company record but not the Evidence Register

The Evidence Register is not a flat list of interesting observations. It is the mechanism by which the Durable Demand Framework evolves based on what the research actually finds. Evidence that contradicts the framework is as valuable as evidence that confirms it — treat disconfirming evidence as a priority finding.

## Success Criteria

This repository is succeeding when:

1. The Evidence Register contains multiple Established findings that have directly updated or created Durable Demand Framework principles
2. The client diagnostic workflow can match a presenting situation to historical evidence with stated confidence
3. Probabilistic statements can be made about survival probability for specific content archetypes: "Based on N comparable pages across M companies, pages with this archetype historically survived at X%"
4. The fragility diagnosis is reliable enough to deliver proactively — to growing clients, before decline occurs

## Four Evidence Dimensions

Every completed company record should preserve evidence for these four dimensions wherever the evidence exists. Not every record will contain all four — some companies never responded; others have not had enough time for outcomes to emerge. Never speculate or fill gaps to complete the framework. The repository accumulates these dimensions across many independent companies; over time that enables evidence-backed prescriptions, not just historical explanation.

| Dimension | Question | Primary template location |
|-----------|----------|--------------------------|
| **Diagnosis** | What caused the traffic movement? | Mechanism section (Primary Mechanism, Pathway, Confidence, Rationale, Disconfirming Evidence) |
| **Response** | What did the company change, if anything? | Recovery section → Observed Company Response |
| **Outcome** | What happened afterward? | Recovery section (Recovery Status, Recovery Mechanism) + Traffic Trajectory (Current Direction) |
| **Applicability** | Under what conditions should another company consider the same approach? | Client Applicability section (Best-Fit Client Type, Relevance Conditions, Non-Comparable When, Primary Lesson) |

**Why these dimensions matter together:** Diagnosis alone explains history. Diagnosis + Response + Outcome enables prescriptions. A record that documents a company recovering without any content intervention teaches a fundamentally different lesson from one where recovery followed a deliberate rewrite program — and the index must capture that distinction to make it retrievable.

**The chain:** Diagnosis → Response → Outcome → Applicability. The Response dimension connects mechanism to recovery in a way the index can surface. Without it, "how have companies with this mechanism historically recovered?" is unanswerable from the data.

## Next Study Selection

When asked what to study next, inspect the current repository state and identify the most important evidence gap. Do not default to the most interesting, dramatic, or convenient company. Study selection should progressively improve the breadth, balance, falsifiability, and quantitative usefulness of the repository.

### What to inspect

Before recommending a next study, consider the current distribution across:

- Outcome classification (Grew / Flat / Declined)
- Primary Mechanism (Algorithm Update / Algorithmic Reassessment / Demand Expansion / etc.)
- Vertical / category
- Company size (SMB / Mid-Market / Enterprise)
- Dominant content model
- Traffic Trajectory (which trajectories are underrepresented)
- Page Archetype coverage in existing PDAs
- AI Substitutability distribution in existing PDAs
- Intervention Level coverage (what intervention types have few observations)
- Observed Page Response coverage (what page-level behaviors are underdocumented)
- AIO exposure and effect (what AIO outcome states need more cases)
- Rate Eligible page observations — how many `Observation Unit = Page, Rate Eligibility = Yes` rows exist and what archetypes/mechanisms are underrepresented in that subset
- Evidence Register findings with few supporting cases
- Evidence Register findings that have not yet faced a challenging/disconfirming case

### Governing principle

Recommend the next study that most reduces an important uncertainty, imbalance, or evidence gap — not simply another interesting case.

More companies alone do not guarantee a less biased dataset. If existing companies cluster around recovery cases in one vertical, another recovery case in the same vertical adds less than a declining company, a stable company, or a company from an underrepresented category.

Be willing to recommend:
- A boring or stable company
- A declining company with no recovery
- A counterexample to an emerging hypothesis
- A company from an underrepresented vertical or size band
- A company with a content model the repository has not yet studied
- A company with confirmed intervention (to test intervention effectiveness)
- A company with no observed intervention (to test whether intervention is necessary)
- A company that could contribute Rate Eligible page observations in an underrepresented archetype
- A specific study type or mechanism rather than a named company, when the evidence gap is structural rather than company-specific

### Falsification

Existing hypotheses should actively generate searches for disconfirming evidence, not only supporting evidence.

If an Evidence Register finding has multiple supporting cases but few or no challenging cases, the most valuable next study may be one capable of bounding or disproving that finding — not one that adds another supporting case.

Example: if Methodological pages repeatedly appear resilient, a declining company with a substantial Methodological page population is a stronger next test than another successful Methodological page from another recovery case.

### Rate Eligibility and Sampling Frame

As the repository matures, consider whether the next study can contribute Rate Eligible observations (`Observation Unit = Page, Rate Eligibility = Yes`) in Page Archetype or AI Substitutability categories that are currently underrepresented. A PDA that establishes a complete and defined sampling frame — not just top-N selections — contributes more to eventual quantitative analysis.

**Governing rule:** Rate Eligibility establishes whether an observation may participate in denominator-based analysis. Sampling Frame establishes which denominator it belongs to. Cross-company page-level rates may only combine observations drawn from compatible sampling frames or from a common inclusion rule applied consistently across all included companies.

Incompatible sampling frames — for example, "top 20% by traffic gain" from one PDA pooled with "top 5 by absolute gain" from another — must not be combined into a single rate calculation. When building cross-company rates, confirm that the inclusion rules are equivalent before pooling.

Do not select companies solely to manufacture a desired quantitative result. The objective is a more defensible denominator, not a particular outcome.

### Recommendation format

When asked what to study next, return:

**Recommended next study:** Specific company if justified, otherwise the type of company or case to find.

**Why this is the highest-value addition:** The imbalance, uncertainty, or evidence gap it addresses.

**What it tests:** Relevant Evidence Register finding, hypothesis, mechanism, archetype, or open question.

**What would strengthen the current finding:** State the observable result.

**What would weaken or challenge it:** State the observable result.

**Dataset contribution:** New coverage added, including whether it could contribute Rate Eligible observations and in which categories.

**Priority:** High / Medium / Low.

Do not force a named company if the repository only supports recommending a study profile. In that case, describe the type of candidate to look for.

## The Tests

Apply these as filters whenever evaluating whether something belongs in the repository:

**The research test:** Does this field, workflow, analysis, or artifact help us better understand durability or make better client recommendations? If neither, it probably doesn't belong.

**The client test:** Would a client pay for this insight? If not, it may be intellectually interesting but strategically unimportant — note it in the record, but don't build infrastructure around it.
