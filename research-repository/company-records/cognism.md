## Risk Flags

- **Homepage dependence** — homepage accounts for 28.3% of current estimated traffic; any further erosion of homepage visibility would disproportionately affect overall traffic
- **Position 51+ near-total collapse** — positions 51+ went from 33,528 (cohort start) to 15 (cohort end), a −99.96% reduction; virtually all long-tail keyword coverage has been eliminated
- **No recovery signal** — traffic still declining at cohort end with no reversal visible in available data; trough has not been established; bottom remains unknown
- **International subfolder exposure** — /de/ and /fr/ subfolders contributed 7.5% of observed page-level gross loss; international performance is not separately tracked and may involve distinct local algorithm dynamics
- **Two-wave attribution risk** — the two inflection events (Wave 1 May–Aug 2025 and Wave 2 Oct 2025) may reflect distinct mechanisms; treating them as one event in client conversations could obscure the Oct 2025 driver

## Narrative

### Company Overview

Cognism is a B2B sales intelligence platform providing contact data, intent signals, and company data for outbound sales and marketing teams. Their organic search strategy is anchored in a large library of definitional and educational content — root-level pages covering "what is X" topics (B2B sales, lead generation, prospecting, revenue operations, go-to-market strategy) alongside an extensive editorial blog covering sales and marketing playbooks. This content model serves Cognism's core audience — SDRs, account executives, and revenue operations teams — and generates substantial non-branded organic traffic from informational queries at the top of the B2B demand funnel.

At the cohort start (Jan 2025), Cognism estimated 223,211 monthly organic visitors. Branded traffic accounted for approximately 14% (31,629) and non-branded informational queries drove the remaining 86% (183,120). International presence through /de/ and /fr/ subfolders is visible in the Top Pages data, though US-indexed traffic dominates the Ahrefs export. Referring domains stood at 3,547 at cohort start — a number that more than doubled by cohort end.

### Traffic Trajectory

Cognism's cohort window traces an early peak followed by a two-wave collapse. Traffic grew from 223,211 (Jan 2025) to a peak of 280,863 in April 2025 (+25.8% from cohort start) before the decline began.

**Wave 1 (May–August 2025):** Traffic fell from 280,863 to 183,232 (−34.8% from peak) over four months. The decline was gradual but consistent. Position counts in the 51+ bucket fell from ~49,639 at peak to ~28,298 by August 2025. The homepage and pricing page showed limited change during this period, and branded traffic remained stable.

**Wave 2 (October 2025 inflection):** A discrete and sharper contraction is visible in October 2025. Ahrefs-estimated ranking page count dropped a net 247 pages in a single month (1,220 → 973, −20.2%). Positions 51+ collapsed 77.5% in one month (22,835 → 5,142). The abruptness of this contraction distinguishes Wave 2 from Wave 1's gradual erosion — it reflects a discrete algorithmic event rather than continuing decay.

From October 2025 through June 2026, traffic continued declining from 170,783 to 110,061. At cohort end, positions 51+ stood at 15 (from 33,528 at cohort start — −99.96%). Positions 11–20 fell from 16,780 to 1,458 (−91.3%). Positions 21–50 fell from 28,939 to 530 (−98.2%). The near-elimination of mid- and long-tail ranking coverage is the defining structural outcome of the cohort window. Peak-to-end decline was −60.8%.

### Mechanism Analysis

The available evidence most strongly supports Algorithmic Reassessment as the primary mechanism — a selective, content-type-specific event rather than a domain-wide authority or demand problem.

The three strongest pieces of evidence: first, 99.0% of the total cohort-window traffic decline is non-branded, with branded traffic effectively flat (−0.4%). This weighs strongly against brand erosion, a sitewide penalty, and any narrative that implicates the domain's overall authority. Second, referring domains grew +82.5% during the same period traffic declined by half — weighing strongly against broad domain-level link loss as a contributing cause. Third, the losses are structurally selective: root-level definitional pages and blog content account for ~92.5% of observed page-level gross loss, while the homepage grew (+7,950 estimated visits gain over the URL comparison window), the pricing page held and grew slightly (+13.7%), and new competitor pricing comparison blog posts gained traction. That pattern — certain URL classes declining sharply while others on the same domain hold or improve — is inconsistent with a domain-wide event.

The two-wave structure provides further evidence. Wave 1 (May–Aug 2025) looks like a gradual algorithmic erosion of long-tail non-branded rankings across the mid- and lower-position buckets. Wave 2 (Oct 2025) is a discrete, single-month contraction affecting estimated page count and position-bucket coverage simultaneously. These two shapes suggest potentially distinct algorithm actions — possibly affecting different layers of the ranking stack — and should be treated as separate events when the update-calendar mapping is done.

ZoomInfo's partial category decline (−19.1%, US-scoped) introduces some category-level headwind as a competing explanation. However, Cognism's decline is more than twice as steep, and the within-domain selectivity of the losses — homepage and pricing resisting while definitional and blog content collapsed — is not consistent with a category-wide demand contraction affecting all URL types equally.

AIO interception is not the primary driver based on the available evidence. In this seven-query sample, AIO was present on all queries. Cognism appeared as a cited AIO source on 3 queries where it still maintains strong organic rankings; it was absent from both AIO and organic results on 3 queries where it has lost ranking presence entirely. In this sample, citation tracks ranking position — which is more consistent with algorithmic de-ranking as the primary cause than with AIO independently displacing previously-ranked pages. However, the sample is too small to infer a general citation rule. The evidence weighs against AIO displacement as a sufficient explanation for the ranking collapse; it cannot rule out AIO contribution as a secondary factor on queries where Cognism has already lost organic presence. The position-bucket cascade (positions 51+ from 33,528 to 15) is characteristic of ranking loss, not click diversion.

Neither wave has been formally mapped to a named Google update. Until that mapping is done, Algorithmic Reassessment is the correct classification. If a credible alignment is established — particularly for the Oct 2025 Wave 2 contraction — the label should be upgraded to Algorithm Update per the repository taxonomy.

### Page & Content Analysis

Current traffic is concentrated. The top 10 pages account for 48.3% of estimated organic traffic at cohort end. The homepage is the dominant single page at 28.3% (30,583 estimated visits) — homepage share rose within a footprint where informational coverage contracted, rather than through intentional homepage optimization.

Across the URL comparison window (~Jul 2024 – Jun 2026), the content-type breakdown of gross loss shows near-total concentration in two categories: root-level definitional and educational pages (48.6%, 50,528 traffic lost across 134 URLs) and editorial blog content (43.9%, 45,656 traffic lost across 247 URLs). International subfolders contributed a combined 7.5% of gross loss. The single largest blog loss was /blog/what-is-a-go-to-market-strategy (−6,620 estimated visits) — a page also confirmed absent from both organic results and AIO on targeted Ahrefs SERP Overview validation (2026-08-03). At the 2026-08-03 snapshot, the page appeared in neither the organic results nor the AIO source set.

Gain decomposition: 62.1% of gross gain came from 146 improving continuing URLs, and 37.9% from 188 new URLs. The most notable new-URL gains are competitor pricing comparison posts (/blog/apollo-io-pricing, /blog/rocketreach-pricing) — a commercial-intent comparison play that appears to be gaining traction. The homepage also gained substantially (+7,950, the single largest individual gaining page). This pattern — competition comparison content and the homepage gaining while definitional educational content collapses — may represent an involuntary content strategy pivot driven by where ranking visibility survived rather than deliberate repositioning.

At 267.1% gross-loss offset rate, replacement activity is real but structurally insufficient. For every 1 estimated visit gained, 2.67 were lost across the comparison window.

### Recovery

No recovery is visible in the cohort window. Traffic declined continuously from the October 2025 inflection through June 2026, with no monthly reversal in the Performance History data. The trough has not been established — the June 2026 figure (110,061) does not represent a floor.

Replacement activity is underway: new competitor comparison content is gaining, and the homepage is growing. Whether this activity represents a durable content pivot or simply reflects survival of the fittest within the existing portfolio is not determinable from the available window.

### Client Applicability

Cognism is a strong cautionary case for B2B SaaS companies with content programs built on informational and definitional pages. The case demonstrates that a mature, well-resourced content operation — with configured brand tracking, nearly doubling referring domains, stable branded traffic, and a large page library — can lose more than half of its organic footprint through two-wave algorithmic reassessment of specific content types.

The most applicable diagnostic pattern: if a client's organic traffic is declining while branded traffic is flat and referring domains are growing, the mechanism is more likely to be a content-type-specific algorithmic event than a domain-wide authority or link problem. Cognism provides a clear, fully-computed example of what that pattern looks like across 18 months of data.

The AIO finding adds a nuanced secondary insight. In this seven-query sample, Cognism was cited in AI Overviews on queries where it still holds top-10 organic rankings, and absent from both organic and AIO on queries where it has lost ranking presence. In this sample, citation tracks ranking position rather than replacing it. For clients concerned about AIO impact on informational content, Cognism illustrates one observable pattern: pages retaining strong organic rankings also appearing in AIO; de-ranked pages absent from both surfaces. This is not a universal finding — it reflects a specific seven-query snapshot and should be presented with that scope.
