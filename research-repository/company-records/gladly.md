**AIO field definitions:**

*AIO Portfolio Susceptibility:*
- **High:** Large share of the site's key queries are informational or answer-oriented — structurally likely to trigger AI Overviews.
- **Medium:** Mixed portfolio; some informational exposure, some navigational or commercial queries.
- **Low:** Query portfolio is primarily navigational, branded, or commercial — low structural exposure.
- **Unknown:** Portfolio-level review not performed.

*AIO Presence Observed:*
- **Yes:** An AI Overview was directly confirmed on at least one key query.
- **No:** Relevant queries were tested and no AI Overview appeared.
- **Not tested:** No query-level AIO review was performed.

*AIO Observed Effect:*
- **Resistant:** A page holds ranking and estimated traffic despite an AI Overview being displayed on that query.
- **Intercepted:** Estimated traffic loss pattern is consistent with AI Overview click interception.
- **Mixed:** Evidence of both resistance and interception across different queries or pages.
- **No conclusion:** Data is insufficient to characterize the effect.
- **Not tested:** AIO presence was not directly observed; effect cannot be assessed.

### Findings

The strongest GSC evidence is property-level: from October 2025 to June 2026, impressions grew from 749,128 to 1,106,689 (+47.7%), average position improved slightly from 12.61 to 12.07, CTR fell from 1.07% to 0.42%, and clicks declined from 7,997 to 4,688 (−41.4%). Search visibility held and improved; clicks declined sharply. This pattern is consistent with search-result satisfaction of informational queries without requiring a click.

At the page level, /blog/faq-template/ accumulated 896,401 impressions with 427 clicks (0.05% CTR, avg position 12.28) and /blog/best-customer-service-software/ accumulated 891,232 impressions with 487 clicks (0.05% CTR, avg position 16.94) over the study window. These lifetime CTR figures reflect average positions of 12–17, where low CTR is expected; they do not establish a period-over-period pattern of CTR collapse on stable-ranking pages without a time-comparative page or query export.

Blog-wide average CTR across the study window was 0.34%.

### Interpretation

The property-level visibility–click divergence is the primary supporting evidence for AI-mediated demand erosion: impressions grew 47.7% while clicks fell 41.4%, with average position slightly improving over the same period. The content types generating the most impression volume — FAQ templates, software comparison lists, call center scripts — are the categories most structurally exposed to AI search-result satisfaction. AI Overview interception is the plausible mechanism, but it has not been directly tested. A query-level comparison for the top exposed pages (October 2025 vs June 2026, including CTR and position) followed by direct SERP testing would determine whether this becomes a verified AIO interception case or remains a broader CTR-erosion case with AIO as the leading hypothesis.

## Risk Flags

- **Domain migration**: gladly.com migrated to gladly.ai ~May 2025. No GSC or Ahrefs data for gladly.com is available, making pre-migration baseline unknown. Post-peak decline is the primary analyzed trajectory, but migration-related effects (redirect stability, canonical issues, query-coverage shifts) cannot be fully isolated without old-domain data.
- **Brand dependence**: Of clicks represented in the GSC query export, approximately 68% are branded ("gladly" queries, all variants). The query export does not capture all property clicks; this figure characterizes the represented top-query rows, not necessarily all organic clicks.
- **Commercial misalignment**: Non-brand organic content (scripts, templates, job descriptions, FAQ content) is structurally disconnected from Gladly's commercial product (AI-powered customer service platform). The content attracts practitioners looking for templates, not buyers evaluating customer service software.
- **Organic minority channel**: Organic search accounts for ~13.9% of total sessions. Gladly is primarily a direct/event/brand-driven site. Organic generates the highest absolute key-event count (1,971) but a lower per-session key-event rate than Paid Search and Email.
- **Position-quality confounding**: High lifetime impression volume with low CTR on /blog/faq-template/ (avg position 12.28) and /blog/best-customer-service-software/ (avg position 16.94) partly reflects average positions outside the top 10, where low CTR is expected. These figures should not be used as standalone evidence of AIO interception.
- **Query-export incompleteness**: The branded/non-branded split is computed from clicks represented in the GSC query export (top rows only). A large share of property clicks are not represented; the split cannot be generalized to all organic traffic.
- **Key-event ambiguity**: GA4 key events have not been broken down by event type. Absolute key-event volume from organic search cannot yet be treated as qualified conversion volume without knowing which actions are counted.
- **Migration validation incomplete**: No redirect map, canonical audit, old-domain GSC property, or URL-level migration validation was available. Migration-related instability at specific URLs cannot be ruled out.

## Narrative

### Company Overview

Gladly is a B2B SaaS customer service platform founded in 2014 and headquartered in San Francisco. The platform centralizes support operations across voice, email, chat, and social channels, built around a "people-based" model that treats customers as individuals rather than ticket numbers. Gladly is venture-backed (Series E, $40M, September 2024) with approximately 230–240 employees. In July 2023, Gladly acquired Thankful.ai, an AI customer support automation company. That acquisition informed a full company rebrand from gladly.com to gladly.ai, completed approximately May 2025, signaling a strategic repositioning around AI-powered customer service.

Gladly's organic content strategy is tool-template-led. The majority of non-brand organic traffic flows from scripts (customer service call scripts, call center scripts), job description templates, FAQ templates, assessment and interview questions, and process guides. This content is structurally disconnected from Gladly's commercial product: it attracts practitioners looking for a template to copy, not buyers evaluating a customer service platform. The domain migration provides a natural study window opening, though the absence of gladly.com historical data prevents pre-migration baseline analysis.

### Traffic Trajectory

Gladly's organic search history on gladly.ai begins at zero. GSC records the first non-zero clicks for the gladly.ai property on May 13, 2025, confirming that gladly.com was the prior domain and the migration completed around that date. No GSC or Ahrefs data for gladly.com is available for this analysis.

Following the migration, traffic ramped rapidly. The first full month (June 2025) recorded 6,945 GSC clicks. This post-migration ramp likely reflects Google processing and consolidating the new domain, though the absence of gladly.com data prevents the migration period from being separated from underlying organic performance. Traffic continued growing through summer and fall, reaching a peak of 7,997 GSC clicks in October 2025.

From that peak, traffic declined consistently. November 2025 dropped to 6,503, December to 5,201. Q1 2026 showed partial stabilization — January 5,885, February 5,618, March 6,312, April 6,329 — before Q2 resumed decline: May 2026 at 5,669, June 2026 at 4,688. The year-over-year change from the first full month (June 2025 to June 2026) is −32.5%. The peak-to-June 2026 decline is −41.4%. July 2026 partial data (2,925 clicks through approximately day 20) projects continued decline.

Over the full study window (May 2025–June 2026), gladly.ai accumulated 81,887 GSC clicks and 13,321,295 impressions per the Chart.csv property-level export.

### Mechanism Analysis

The primary inferred driver is AI-mediated informational demand erosion: a progressive reduction in clicks as informational queries are increasingly satisfied within the search experience before users visit the ranked page. AI Overview interception is the leading pathway hypothesis, but it has not been directly confirmed.

The strongest available evidence is a property-level visibility–click divergence from October 2025 to June 2026. Over that period, impressions grew from 749,128 to 1,106,689 (+47.7%), average position improved slightly from 12.61 to 12.07, CTR fell from 1.07% to 0.42%, and clicks declined from 7,997 to 4,688 (−41.4%). Search visibility held and moderately improved while clicks contracted. This pattern — position stable or improving, impressions growing, clicks and CTR falling — is consistent with search-result satisfaction of informational queries that no longer require a click to resolve.

The content types generating the most impression volume reinforce this interpretation. Of clicks represented in the GSC query export (top rows only), approximately 68% are branded "gladly" queries. The remaining represented non-branded traffic is concentrated in call center scripts, customer service scripts, job description templates, FAQ templates, and assessment content. These are the categories most structurally susceptible to AI-mediated satisfaction: a script, a job description template, or an FAQ can be generated directly in the SERP without requiring the user to visit Gladly's page.

The strongest competing explanation is post-migration normalization or migration-related instability. Without gladly.com data, it is not possible to rule out incomplete redirect consolidation, canonical instability, changed query coverage, or temporary branded-query shifts contributing to the observed trajectory. The improving average position and growing impressions make a simple sitewide ranking collapse less likely, but URL-level migration effects cannot be isolated. The evidence weighs against a pure ranking-loss explanation; it does not definitively exclude migration-related contributors.

Direct AIO presence testing at the query level was not conducted. A query-level comparison for the top exposed pages (October 2025 vs June 2026 clicks, impressions, CTR, and position) followed by SERP testing for highest-impression queries would determine whether this becomes a verified AIO interception case or remains a broader CTR-erosion case with AIO as the primary hypothesis.

### Page & Content Analysis

Gladly's current organic footprint, as measured by Ahrefs (July 2026 snapshot), is top-heavy. The homepage accounts for 20.5% of total estimated organic traffic (1,875 of 9,154 estimated monthly visits). The top five pages represent 46.7% and the top ten represent 66.9%.

The top non-brand pages by estimated Ahrefs traffic are: /blog/customer-service-scripts/ (746), /blog/outsourcing-customer-service/ (598), and /blog/customer-care-representative-job-description/ (560). All are scripts, templates, or job description content.

Two pages accumulate substantial impression volume in GSC. /blog/faq-template/ recorded 896,401 impressions and 427 clicks (0.05% CTR) at an average GSC position of 12.28. /blog/best-customer-service-software/ recorded 891,232 impressions and 487 clicks (0.05% CTR) at average position 16.94. At average positions 12–17, low CTR is expected and does not by itself indicate AIO suppression. These figures reflect high impression volume on mid-ranking pages. A period-over-period query or page export would be needed to assess whether CTR declined over time on stable-ranking queries.

GA4 data shows that organic search accounts for 13.9% of total sessions and generates the highest absolute number of key events of any channel (1,971 vs. 1,912 for Direct and 1,878 for Paid Search). However, its per-session key-event rate (approximately 1.1%) is lower than Paid Search (approximately 2.2%) and Email (approximately 3.1%). Page-level GA4 data is not segmented by acquisition channel, so the specific pages driving organic key events cannot be identified from the available data.

Because no Ahrefs comparison data is available, page-level Gross Gain and Loss, Winning and Losing Content Type, and Page Replacement Pattern cannot be determined.

### Recovery

No recovery is evident. GSC clicks declined from 7,997 (October 2025) to 4,688 (June 2026) without reversal. The Q1 2026 pattern suggested brief stabilization, but Q2 resumed decline. July 2026 partial data projects continuation. The supplied datasets do not show evidence of a performance reversal. No content-action inventory was available to determine whether Gladly materially changed its content model during the study period.

### Client Applicability

Gladly illustrates the visibility–click divergence pattern with clear property-level evidence. For clients who believe their organic program is healthy because search visibility is stable, this case offers a direct counter-example: impressions growing and average position improving while clicks contract 41%.

The case is most applicable when a client's content portfolio is dominated by templates, scripts, basic factual definitions, or FAQ-answerable content — the categories most structurally susceptible to search-result satisfaction. It is not applicable to clients whose content requires investment, thought, or application to the user's specific situation.

The commercial misalignment observation is independently useful: the highest-impression organic content (scripts, job descriptions, FAQ templates) attracts practitioners seeking a ready-made asset, not buyers evaluating a customer service platform. Even where organic traffic arrives, the structural intent mismatch limits its contribution to commercial outcomes — though the extent to which organic key events are tied to commercial pages specifically cannot be determined from the available data.
