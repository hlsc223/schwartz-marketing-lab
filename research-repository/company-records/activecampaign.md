### Coverage

### Findings

| Query | SERP Feature | AC Position | AIO Effect |
|-------|-------------|-------------|-----------|
| email marketing campaigns | AIO present (position 1); AC cited within AIO | Organic position 4 | Dual presence — cited in AIO and ranking organically |
| activecampaign pricing | AIO at position 4 (cites help docs + review sites, not pricing page); PAA at position 2 | Organic position 1 | Click interception candidate — AIO does not cite the official pricing page |
| active campaign crm | AIO at position 2 (cites AC's own CRM page) | Organic position 1 | Dual presence — AIO cites AC's page and AC holds organic position 1 |
| company slogans | No AIO | Organic position 1 | No AIO interception on primary query |
| email subject line generator | No AIO | Organic position 7 | Competitive displacement by AI-native tools at positions 1–6 (not AIO) |

### Interpretation

Dual AIO presence on glossary and CRM queries — cited within the AIO while also holding organic rank — suggests content authority sufficient to survive interception on those query types. Pricing is the exception: the AIO appears but routes intent toward help docs and review sites rather than the pricing page, creating click interception even at position 1. Whether current AIO effects contributed to the historical decline or emerged after it cannot be established from a single current snapshot.

## Risk Flags

- **Narrow recovery base:** Two pages account for 49% of recovery-window gross gain. The recovery offset rate (0.962) means continued losses elsewhere nearly cancel the gains. The recovery is not portfolio-wide.
- **Blog net-loss dominated by AI-answerable definitional content:** The blog content type posted the largest net loss in the cohort window (−36,796). Several of the largest individual losers are generic, highly answerable informational pages. The Portfolio Durability Analysis found the aggregate blog loss (−50,662 gross loss, ~3.6× loss-to-gain ratio within the category) is inconsistent with isolated failure — consistent with systematic deprioritization. Full URL-level enumeration of the blog decline cohort remains pending to confirm this at the individual page level.
- **Pricing page: AIO does not cite official pricing source.** The AIO on "activecampaign pricing" (position 4) cites help docs and review sites, not the pricing page. The official pricing page holds position 1 but is not referenced by Google's AI summary for its own primary commercial query. 1,057 keywords dropped from the pricing page's index entirely; replacement analysis not completed.
- **SDR blog: near-complete abandonment of unchanged content.** The `/blog/sales-development-representative` page lost 711 of 716 keywords from the index without a visible content change. The Portfolio Durability Analysis found the aggregate blog decline pattern is consistent with this being systematic rather than isolated. The SDR SERP replacement analysis (confirming which source type now holds those positions) remains the highest-priority pending test.
- **URL migration unadjusted in calculations.** The subject line generator migration (`/free-marketing-tools/subject-line-generator` → `/tools/subject-line-generator`) is confirmed but not reconciled in the Gross Gain/Loss figures. The old URL inflates Gross Loss; the new URL inflates New-URL gain. Impact on company-level metrics is likely small but should be confirmed.
- **Keyword export cap hit at 30,000 rows.** Long-tail keyword traffic is underrepresented. All branded/non-branded split and AIO overlap figures from the keyword export are scoped to the top 30,000 keywords.
- **AIO historical state unconfirmable.** Current SERP data shows AIO on commercial queries but cannot establish when those AI Overviews appeared relative to the decline. The AIO paradox (overlap keywords gaining traffic in the keyword export) is consistent with multiple interpretations.
