## Study Overview

## AI Overview Interception Evidence

*Source: Chapter 9 — What AI Overviews Actually Do to Traffic*

- In the clearest documented cases, pages climbed or held Google's rankings while losing **between 80% and 99%** of the clicks those rankings previously generated
- This pattern appeared across **more than 20 individual queries** at a single company (Aha!) — pages that held or improved their rankings while traffic fell 79–99%
- The pattern is consistent with **AI Overview interception**: Google's AI-generated answers resolving queries before users click through to ranking pages
- Identical traffic declines often stemmed from **different underlying mechanisms**, making accurate diagnosis a prerequisite for effective recovery

### Aha! same-position CTR collapse examples

| Keyword | Previous position | Current position | Traffic change |
|---|---|---|---|
| product marketing | 1 | 1 | −98% |
| marketing job titles | 3 | 3 | −99% |
| user story template | 3 | 1 (improved) | −92% |
| product positioning | 2 | 2 | −95% |
| product roadmap template | 2 | 2 | −93% |

*Current SERP data (July 2026) confirms AI Overviews are active on all five queries above.*

### The five mechanisms behind organic traffic decline

Researchers classified every major traffic decline into one of five mechanisms:

1. Algorithm update damage
2. AI Overview interception
3. Architectural damage
4. AI tool demand substitution
5. Competitive displacement

## Growth Pattern Findings

*Source: Chapter 5 — The Anatomy of Durable Growth*

- Researchers identified a primary growth driver for **38** companies in the cohort (full-weight classifiable growers)
- Among those 38, **61%** grew primarily through **definitional and educational content** — content covering foundational professional concepts professionals return to throughout their careers
- The pattern appeared independently across **five verticals**: HR, DevOps, finance, security, and marketing automation
- In **no growing program** did a single page account for more than roughly **20%** of total gains — growth was distributed across dozens of pages, each contributing modestly
- The distinguishing characteristic: the content required **sustained engagement** to deliver its value — AI systems could not fully satisfy the query without the user reading the page

### Content growth pattern distribution (38 full-weight classifiable growers)

- Definitional / educational content: **23 of 38 (61%)**
- Brand-led growth: 7 of 38 (18%)
- Mixed growth patterns: 7 of 38 (18%)
- Programmatic content: 1 of 38 (3%)

### Companies showing durable definitional content growth

All five grew organic traffic through AI Overview expansion despite industry predictions that informational content would be broadly suppressed:

- **Rippling** (HR / People Operations): +28.0%
- **Navan** (Finance / Spend Management): +36.0%
- **Ramp** (Finance / Spend Management): +112.5%
- **Harness** (Developer Tools / DevOps): +105.8%
- **Vanta** (Security / Compliance): +60.3%

## Key Assumptions Overturned

*Source: Chapter 2 — The Assumptions That Didn't Survive the Data*

**Assumption 1: AI Overviews suppress informational content broadly**
- Five companies in five independent categories grew definitional content throughout the AI Overview expansion window
- Suppression is query-specific (satisfiable queries), not category-wide

**Assumption 2: Traffic growth reflects program health**
- LogRocket: 66% below all-time peak, ARR grew +22% YoY — business and organic program moved in opposite directions
- ZoomInfo: +154.7% traffic, +14.7% traffic value — 140-point divergence from low-intent query growth

**Assumption 3: Concentrated bets drive growth**
- No growing company showed a single page exceeding ~20% of gross gains
- Durable growth was distributed across many pages, not concentrated in a hero asset

**Assumption 4: Publishing more compounds growth**
- **LogRocket cannibalization rate: 75.6%** — for every unit of traffic gained, 0.76 units were lost elsewhere in the portfolio
- **Aha! cannibalization rate: 286.6%** — every unit of traffic created was accompanied by 2.86 units of traffic lost

## AI Citation Testing

*Source: Chapter 10 — Being Found by AI Systems*

Study tested 13 prompts across **6 AI engines**: ChatGPT, Copilot, Google AI Mode, Google AI Overviews, Google Gemini, and Perplexity. Total: **78 engine-prompt combinations**. (Hyperproof and LogRocket were added to the deep-dive cohort after citation testing was complete; 13 of 15 deep-dive companies were tested.)

### Citation outcomes

- **9 of 13 cases**: AI systems cited the target company's domain
- **7 of 9** confirmed citations: the exact target URL was the confirmed citation
- **2 of 9** confirmed citations: cited the domain via a different URL on the same site
- **4 of 13 cases**: zero citation of the target company on any engine tested

### Content types producing citations

- Blog/educational articles: **5 of 9** confirmed citations (most common)
- Glossary/definitional pages: **2 of 9** confirmed citations
- Product pages and marketing content: fewest citations

### Engine-level behavior differences

- **Google AI Mode and Google AI Overviews**: highest citation rates for cohort content — appeared to weight web authority signals more heavily
- **Perplexity**: cited across a broader range of sources; less correlated with domain authority; more likely to cite multiple domains per response, including smaller publishers
- **ChatGPT and Copilot**: lowest citation rates for cohort content; more likely to synthesize from training data without citing specific sources

### Commercial query citations

Three of the cohort's strongest traffic growers returned zero citations across all 6 engines for commercial comparison queries:
- ZoomInfo (+154.7% traffic): 0 of 6 engines for "What are the best B2B sales intelligence platforms?"
- Braze (+87.4% traffic): 0 of 6 engines for "What are the best marketing automation platforms for mobile apps?"
- PostHog (+131.4% traffic): 0 of 6 engines for "What are the best product analytics tools for engineering teams?"

Organic traffic growth and commercial AI citation do not reliably correlate.

### Strongest citation cases

- **CircleCI**: 4 of 6 engines cited — highest citation rate of any company in the cohort. Content cited: technical blog post directly answering a common DevOps concept question.
- **Vanta**: Cited on 4 of 6 engines for "What's the best SOC 2 compliance automation software?" — strongest commercial citation result in the cohort. But Vanta's single largest traffic-gaining page (+480→21,462 traffic, ~45% of net Top Pages gain) was cited by **0 of 6 engines** for "What is a risk management framework?" — because AI systems routed to NIST and regulatory authorities instead.
- **Rippling**: Glossary content was both AIO-resistant in search and AI-cited in this study. Cross-environment confirmation: same content performing well in both SERP and AI contexts.

### Structural routing exceptions

Four of 13 non-citations were not content quality failures — they were structural routing decisions:
- **Operational queries** (e.g., "is [service] down"): AI systems cited dedicated uptime tools (Downdetector, UptimeRobot) across all engines. No editorial content was cited.
- **Institutional authority queries** (e.g., risk management frameworks): AI systems cited NIST, AWS, and regulatory sources. Brand content explaining the same frameworks was not retrieved.

> Not being cited is not always a content quality problem. When AI systems route to specialist tools or institutional authorities, the decision is structural — driven by query type, not content strength.

## Portfolio Health Benchmarks

*Source: Appendix C — Metrics and Calculations; Chapter 11 — The Durable Demand Playbook*

### Cannibalization rate thresholds

| Rate | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| < 30% | Healthy — gains substantially exceed losses |
| 30–60% | Caution zone — losses meaningful but growth still net positive |
| > 60% | Treadmill — production primarily replacing losses, not building new traffic |
| > 100% | Net-negative — gross losses exceed gross gains; program is shrinking |

Examples from the cohort:
- **LogRocket**: 75.6% — for every unit of traffic gained, 0.76 units lost elsewhere. Requires ~4x gross gains to produce 1 unit of net growth.
- **Aha!**: 286.6% — for every unit gained, 2.86 units lost. Every content investment accelerated net decline.

### Single-page concentration thresholds

| Concentration | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| < 20% | Distributed — no single page is load-bearing for program growth |
| 20–35% | Elevated — monitor for dependency |
| > 35% | High concentration risk — a single-page event can reverse program trajectory |

In the growing companies with the cleanest signals, no single page exceeded ~20% of gross gains. Braze, Navan, Harness, Vanta, and Ramp all showed broad distributed growth.

### Branded dependency thresholds

| Branded % of growth | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| < 40% | Growth is primarily content-driven — non-branded compounding |
| 40–60% | Mixed signal — run branded and non-branded trend separately |
| > 60% | Headline primarily reflects product momentum, not content program |

Examples:
- **Gainsight**: ~65% of apparent traffic growth attributable to a single branded keyword. Headline showed a growing organic program; non-branded content program had limited compounding.
- **ZoomInfo**: 76% of traffic growth traced to one page-type; meaningful share traceable to a specific URL pattern. +154.7% headline traffic; +14.7% traffic value.

## Content Type Vulnerability Data

*Source: Chapter 11; Synthesis Matrix*

### Template content collapse (Aha!)

Aha!'s template content library showed the most severe content-format-specific decline in the cohort:
- **154 template pages** with net gross loss of **−60.3%** (vs. −42.4% overall site decline)
- Losses consistent across all template sub-types regardless of topic

| Template page | Traffic before | Traffic after | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Competitor analysis templates | 5,307 | 632 | −88.1% |
| Strategy templates | 3,669 | 373 | −89.8% |
| Creative brief templates | 2,217 | 80 | −96.4% |

- **59.8%** of Aha!'s total traffic losses were on AIO-exposed queries
- **83.6%** of Aha!'s traffic gains were also on AIO-exposed queries (gains concentrated on broad head terms that AI Overviews also serve)

The key differentiator: template content can deliver its full value within the SERP (a list, a framework, a step-by-step process). Definitional and educational content requiring continued engagement showed the opposite pattern — resistance, not suppression.

### Hyperproof (Decline then Recovery)
- Positions 51+ fell from **9,867 to 16** — effectively zero — by June 2026
- Positions 1–3 grew **+263%** from January 2025 to June 2026
- Top positions held throughout the mid/long-tail collapse
- Recovery driven by continued top-position momentum + breakthrough on "risk management frameworks" (56k vol) from position 21 to position 8

### Vanta (Grew +60.3%)
- Largest single traffic-gaining page: +480→21,462 traffic (a ~4,471% gain) = ~45% of net Top Pages gain
- Non-branded traffic: +159.3%; branded traffic: +38.1% — both grew concurrently
- Cited on 4 of 6 AI engines for commercial SOC 2 query; 0 of 6 for GRC/risk framework query

### LogRocket (Within-Window Recovery)
- 3,150 pages dropped to zero traffic during the study window
- Blog net traffic: +2.9% (gross gain +79,887 offset by gross loss -60,388)
- Branded/geographic recovery: ~+191% on "logrocket" branded keyword (geographic expansion)
- Overall within-window recovery: +19.85%
- Historically still 66.3% below August 2023 all-time peak of 368,021 monthly visits

### CloudBees (Grew, Masked Collapse)
- Non-branded traffic: +106.8%
- Branded traffic: **−16.6%** — brand demand eroded while generic tutorial content grew
- Multi-bucket long-tail collapse: positions 11-20/21-50/51+ each fell 60-75% in one month (aligned to August 2025 spam update)
- The headline growth number masked both branded erosion and long-tail collapse

### Ramp (Grew +112.5%)
- 5 major funding/valuation events across the study window — densest funding cadence in the cohort
- Sharpest single monthly traffic jump: **87% non-branded** — funding announcements not the primary growth driver
- ~22% of gross gains offset by losses (1,234 losing pages) — meaningful cannibalization despite strong headline growth
- Long-tail positions 21-50/51+ each collapsed ~90% in three months aligned to August 2025 spam update; collapse fully masked by top-position growth in headline numbers

## Validated Research Principles

*Source: Synthesis Matrix — Phase 3 findings at Validated status*

Three growth principles reached Validated status (appeared independently across 5+ companies in distinct categories and contexts):

1. **Broad-based distributed content growth** — confirmed in 6 independent cases: Gainsight, Braze, Navan, Harness, Vanta, Ramp. No single page above ~20% of gross gains. Programs chasing hero assets showed opposite risk profile.

2. **Ranking position consolidation toward the SERP head** — confirmed in 6 cases: Rippling, Navan, Harness, Vanta, CloudBees, Ramp. Long-tail positions (21-50, 51+) collapse while top-1-10 positions grow. Pattern is specific to content-driven growth trajectories.

3. **Definitional/educational content resisting AI Overview suppression** — confirmed in 5 cases: Rippling, Navan, Harness, Vanta, Ramp. The same content types that held in search also received the most AI citations. Suppression is query-specific (satisfiable queries), not category-wide.
