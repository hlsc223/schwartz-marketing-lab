## 3. KEY FINDINGS

**Section label:** What the Data Showed

**Finding 1**
Of 118 companies tracked, 47% declined and 37% grew, often in the same vertical competing for the same keywords.

**Finding 2**
The strongest differentiator we observed wasn't company size, domain authority, publishing volume, or backlink growth. It was content composition: whether a program's core traffic assets created ongoing engagement or satisfied a one-time query.

**Finding 3**
Among content-driven growing companies, 61% built primarily on definitional and conceptual content: the kind that generates demand that renews itself as new people enter roles where those concepts matter.

**Finding 4**
In several companies we observed an unusual pattern: rankings held, or even improved, while traffic on those same pages fell dramatically. The most acute case showed traffic falling 80–99% on pages that were simultaneously moving up in position. The pattern is consistent with click interception occurring directly on the results page, before the user reaches the site.

**Finding 5**
The most consistent predictor of AI Overview exposure was satisfiability: whether a user could get what they needed from the AI response alone, without clicking through. Content that required engagement to be useful generally held up better. Content that could be extracted and delivered in a single response proved more vulnerable.

## 4. WHY THIS RESEARCH MATTERS

**Section label:** Why This Research Exists

Much organic growth advice is built on opinion, single case studies, or platform-specific recommendations that reflect the interests of whoever is publishing them.

This study doesn't ask what practitioners recommend. It asks what actually happened across 118 companies, simultaneously, over 18 months, during the period when AI-era search pressure became impossible to ignore.

The goal was to find out what the data showed, including where it contradicted common assumptions.

## 6. METHODOLOGY

**Section label:** About the Research

The study tracked 118 B2B SaaS companies from January 2025 to June 2026 using Ahrefs organic traffic data, keyword-level position exports, and traffic value estimates. Companies were selected across six categories: Developer Tools, Security and Compliance, Product Analytics, Customer Success, Finance and Spend Management, and Marketing Automation. Sixteen companies received full deep-dive analysis; the remaining cohort informed the quantitative distribution findings.

The study was observational. Findings describe patterns in the data and the mechanisms most consistent with those patterns, not controlled outcomes.
