# Why This Article Exists

B2B SaaS companies watching their organic traffic decline in 2025–2026 are working from an incomplete explanation. Most have attributed the loss to "AI" as a single undifferentiated cause, which produces an equally undifferentiated response. This article documents what S.LAB's 118-company cohort study actually found: traffic didn't decline uniformly — it split. Some programs grew substantially while others lost half their traffic in the same categories, targeting the same queries. The article establishes the finding, names the three forces that drove it, and introduces the content durability distinction that determined which side of the split a program landed on. It fills the gap between "AI is disrupting SEO" (widely said, poorly evidenced) and a mechanism-level explanation backed by primary research.

---

# Unique Contribution

The best existing coverage of AI's effect on B2B SEO is either anecdotal, based on aggregated industry surveys, or focused on single-company case studies. No public research has classified 118 companies at the mechanism level across an 18-month window and documented what specifically differentiated growing from declining programs. The cohort data — particularly the Aha! same-position CTR collapse evidence (20+ queries, −80–99% traffic while rankings held or improved) and the 61% definitional content growth pattern across five independent verticals — is not available anywhere else. This article introduces that evidence into public discourse.

# Why Are We the Right People to Write This?

S.LAB ran the study. We screened 195 companies, selected 118 across 10 verticals, classified each company's organic traffic trajectory using a standardized methodology, and performed mechanism-level deep dives on 16 companies. The research repository contains company records, comparison briefs, a synthesis matrix, and three rounds of algorithm update and AI Overview assessments. This is first-party original research — not a synthesis of other people's data.
