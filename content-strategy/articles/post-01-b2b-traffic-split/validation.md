It's a solo practitioner POV, not primary research.

**What's differentiated about our article:**

Every current research publication treats organic traffic decline as a **collective phenomenon** — something that happened broadly to most companies. The 73% decline statistic (used by multiple sources) reinforces this frame. Nobody in the top results is reporting that 37% of companies *grew* over the same period, or explaining why growth and decline happened simultaneously in the same categories.

The SERP is full of "here's why traffic declined and what to do about it." There is no "here's why some companies grew while others declined — and here's the specific mechanism behind the split."

That gap is real and unoccupied.

## 4. Information Gap Assessment

**What exists and is covered well:**

- Aggregate CTR decline data from AI Overviews (Ahrefs, Seer, DataSlayer — rigorous)
- Industry-level traffic decline statistics (The Digital Bloom, KEO Marketing)
- Advisory content on adapting to AI search ("what to do about it" genre)
- The "rankings held, traffic fell" observation is out there (Austin Heaton) but without primary research backing it

**What is missing entirely:**

- Primary research at the company level showing a split between growers and decliners
- The counterintuitive finding that 37% grew — this is completely absent from the SERP
- Mechanism-level classification with distinct signatures per mechanism
- Evidence that content structure (not just "quality") is the predictor — specifically whether content requires engagement vs. delivers a one-time answer

**What is incorrect or oversimplified:**

- The "organic traffic declined uniformly" narrative is the dominant frame and is factually incomplete
- "AI Overviews caused the decline" treats one mechanism as the only mechanism
- Algorithm updates are not being discussed separately from AI Overview interception — they produce different data signatures and require different responses
